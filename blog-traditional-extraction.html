From Palm to Press: How We Cold-Extract Coconut Oil

Kavinda Perera Head of Regenerative Agroforestry • Published Oct 18, 2026
8 min read

In modern industrial agriculture, speed and volume have replaced care and patience. Most consumers do not realize that commodity coconut oil is produced using "dry expeller processing" of copra—coconut meat that has been left to dry in open-air pits or smoke kilns for days, often developing mold and bacterial colonies.

At COCONEST, our extraction philosophy is fundamentally different: we utilize exclusively the fresh wet-milling centrifugal method. Here is how that process unfolds over the course of four critical hours.

Hour 01: The Dawn Hand Harvest

Our harvesting crews climb tree-by-tree in Negombo’s biodiverse agroforestry estates. We do not harvest fallen nuts; fallen nuts may have cracked upon ground impact, allowing airborne microbes to trigger premature acidity. Instead, harvesters cut only prime 11-12 month mature green/brown bunches.

Hour 02: De-Husking & Spring Water Washes

Once gathered at the estate sorting station, the thick fibrous husk is removed using traditional steel spikes. The inner shell is gently cracked, and the coconut water is collected for nutrient reclamation. The thin brown skin (testa) is pared off so only the pure white inner kernel enters the expeller.

Hour 03: The Chilled Expeller Press

The fresh kernel is finely shredded and fed through continuous stainless steel expeller screws equipped with chilled-water cooling jackets. While friction naturally generates heat, our active cooling keeps the mash temperature strictly below 38°C (100.4°F), preserving delicate heat-sensitive enzymes.

Hour 04: Centrifugal Separation at 12,000 RPM

The resulting emulsion—creamy, fresh organic coconut milk—is immediately pumped into our German-engineered tubular centrifuges. Operating at 12,000 RPM, centrifugal force exploits the slight density differential between pure coconut oil, water, and milk proteins. In less than 180 seconds, crystal-clear virgin coconut oil flows from the upper spout into pharmaceutical nitrogen-flushed holding tanks.
